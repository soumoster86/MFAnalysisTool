# Fund costs and structure

## Expense ratio (TER)

The annual percentage of your investment that the AMC deducts to run the fund.
It is not billed separately — it is already subtracted from the NAV you see, so
published returns are always net of the expense ratio.

SEBI caps TER on a sliding scale that falls as the fund's AUM grows. Typical
ranges in India:

- Direct equity funds: roughly 0.5% – 1.2%
- Regular equity funds: roughly 1.5% – 2.25%
- Index funds and ETFs: roughly 0.1% – 0.5%
- Liquid and overnight funds: roughly 0.1% – 0.3%

The gap between Direct and Regular is distributor commission. Over long
horizons it compounds into a large sum: 1% a year on a 20-year SIP typically
costs a mid-single-digit percentage of the final corpus.

A high TER is only justified if the fund reliably delivers alpha above it.
Expense ratio is a certainty; alpha is not.

## Direct vs Regular plans

Same portfolio, same manager, same NAV movement — different cost. Direct plans
have no distributor commission and therefore a lower TER and a higher NAV over
time. If you choose funds yourself, there is no reason to hold Regular plans.

## Exit load

A fee charged for redeeming before a minimum holding period, typically 1% if
you exit within a year on equity funds. It is deducted from the redemption
amount. Liquid funds often have a graded load over the first week or none.

## Portfolio turnover

The percentage of the portfolio traded over a year. Turnover of 100% means the
manager replaced holdings equivalent to the entire book.

High turnover implies higher transaction costs and brokerage borne by the fund
(these sit outside the TER and are not visible in it), and in some structures
worse tax efficiency. It is not automatically bad — a genuinely active strategy
trades — but persistent high turnover with no alpha to show for it is a cost
being paid for nothing.

## AUM

Assets under management. Very small AUM raises viability questions and can
worsen liquidity. Very large AUM in a small-cap fund is a real constraint: the
manager cannot enter or exit positions without moving the price, which pushes
them toward larger names and quietly changes the fund's character. AUM matters
most in small-cap, least in index funds.

## Riskometer

SEBI's mandated risk label, from Low through Low to Moderate, Moderate,
Moderately High, High, to Very High. It is recalculated monthly from the
portfolio's actual composition, so a change in the riskometer is a genuine
signal that what the fund holds has shifted.

## IDCW vs Growth

IDCW (Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal, formerly "dividend") pays out
periodically, and the NAV falls by the amount distributed. Growth reinvests
everything and the NAV compounds.

IDCW is not extra income — it is your own capital returned to you, and it is
taxed at your slab rate. For most long-term investors Growth is the correct
choice; if you need cash flow, a systematic withdrawal plan from a Growth plan
is usually more tax-efficient than IDCW.

## Fund manager tenure

How long the current manager has run the fund. A long track record makes past
performance attributable to the person still making decisions. A recent manager
change means the historical numbers were produced by someone else, and the
fund's future may not resemble its past — which is why this app raises a
critical alert when the manager on record changes.
